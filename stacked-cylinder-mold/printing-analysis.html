2 · How much strength is actually needed

Log scale. The forces on a core during a pour and a pull are tiny next to what even a thin PLA shell can carry. Parts don't fail by being pulled apart; they fail locally.

Braz ring core: Ø94 mm, ~270 cm² in contact with plaster. Release pull assumes 5 kPa of grip (with mold soap it is usually less). Shell capacity: 1.2 mm wall × circumference × ~50 MPa.

Where parts really fail

Prying with a tool

A screwdriver dents a 1.2 mm skin. Give force a place to go instead: jack screws and pry notches with local thickness.

The core-to-plate joint

All the pull ends up here. One thick collar is cheaper than a thick core.

Layer delamination where you grip

Fingers or clamps on thin walls peel layers apart. Grip on ears, flanges, plate rims.

Heat while the plaster sets

A stressed part (bolted sleeve, wide flat plate) can creep or warp in the 45–50 °C window. See below.

The heat window

Plaster heats as it sets. A thick piece (the app warns above ~1 L) peaks around 45–50 °C for 20–40 minutes. PLA's glass transition is ~55–60 °C, but it starts to creep under sustained load a little below that. Thin parts with plaster only on one side shed heat and are fine. Big flat plates and bolted sleeves are the ones at risk.

  • Mix with cool water: slower set, lower peak.
  • Don't over-tighten sleeve bolts; snug is enough.
  • If one spool of something else is worth buying, make it PETG for the universal tooling (Tg ~80 °C).

3 · Make release easy instead of making parts strong

Plaster doesn't bond to PLA chemically. It grips in three ways, and each is cheaper to defeat than to out-muscle.

1 · Mechanical keying into layer lines

Fix: mold soap (3 coats) or a thin wax, every pour. Fine layers (0.12–0.16 mm) on surfaces that form the ware; those layer lines also print onto every piece you cast.

2 · Suction on flat faces

Fix: blow air through the lid's bleed holes. On bed plates, add flush jack screws (M5 in heat-set inserts) that push the plaster off evenly.

3 · Low draft

Comes from the ware itself; the slicer already turns no-draft bands into splits. Setting expansion slightly loosens plaster from male cores; female features (sleeve, key cones) are handled by the split sleeve and 40° cone sides.

4 · Recommended approach: three tiers, printed differently

A ring unit, exploded. Colours show which tier each part belongs to.

Tier 1 Universal tooling · print once per cylinder Ø

Bed plates, lids, sleeve halves. Sturdy, coarse layers (0.24–0.28 mm): their surfaces form parting faces and the outside of the cylinder, not the ware. PETG if you buy one other material. Reused by every unit of every mold of that diameter.

Tier 2 Ware-specific · print lean, print fine

Cores, footer stubs, split inserts, the well's slit tube. Separate each core from its plate; it registers in a socket on the universal plate. Hollow: 3 walls (~1.2 mm), 5–10% gyroid, solid top cap, 0.12–0.16 mm adaptive layers on the forming surface. A thicker collar only at the joint.

Tier 3 Release hardware · designed in

Jack screw bosses in plates and lids, pry notches at the sleeve ears, air ports. These are where force goes, so only these get local thickness. Everything else stays thin.

Removing height-specific sleeves

Today the lid sits on the sleeve rim, so every unit height needs its own sleeve pair (the Braz mold prints three pairs, ~306 g). The core always ends flush with the plaster's top face. A lid that drops inside the sleeve and rests on the core top lands at exactly the right height for any unit. Then one tall sleeve pair per diameter serves every unit.

Trade-offs: a little plaster flash where the lid slides in the sleeve; footer and well collar (no lid) pour to a fill mark instead of the rim. Standard heights plus spacer rings is the alternative.

5 · Standard cylinder diameters, including plates

Universal tooling only pays off if molds share a diameter. Each form below sits at the diameter the slicer fits it to (lip or widest point + ¼″ ledge + 25 mm plaster); arrows show where it lands in the proposed series. The Braz plate (Ø282 × 25 mm) needs a Ø350 cylinder. On your Bambu H2S (340 × 320 × 340 mm) everything up to Ø300 prints whole; Ø350 needs tiled tooling.

H2S bed 340 × 320 mm. A round part is limited by the 320 mm depth: the bed plate is Ø + 20 mm and the lid Ø + 15 mm, so Ø300 is the largest size whose whole kit prints in one piece (sleeve halves, Ø + 26 mm long, lie along the 340 mm axis). On a 256 mm printer the limit would be Ø230.

Two families

Printed whole on the H2S · Ø150 / 175 / 200 / 230 / 280

Cups, mugs, tumblers, vases, bowls, and side plates up to ~Ø215. The three-tier approach as described: universal plates, lids and sleeves printed once per size. Ø280 is new: it fits the H2S, not a 256 mm printer.

Tiled · Ø350 · dinner plates, platters

  • Bed plate and lid as printed tiles. The Ø370 bed plate splits into three 120° tiles (each ~320 × 95 mm, tight on the H2S) or four quadrants (~262 mm, comfortable), keyed and bolted together.
  • Walls you don't print. Plate molds are shallow (~85 mm stack), so the sleeve can be a cottle: a flexible plastic strip wrapped to size and strapped. Ø350 is also almost exactly a 14″ form tube. Printed arc segments are the alternative.
  • The plate core prints whole. The Braz plate is Ø282, under the H2S's 320 mm, so the ware-forming surface has no seam.

Plates want two pieces, not three

A plate flares all the way to its rim, so a one-piece footer cup releases it upward. Today the slicer cuts at the foot crease (10 mm) and leaves a 15 mm ring for the rim, just at the sliver limit. A rule that folds short rings into the footer when the footer can release them would give plates footer + collar: two plaster pieces.

Decisions for you

Standard cylinder diameters: Ø150 / 175 / 200 / 230 / 280 printed whole on the H2S, plus Ø350 for dinner plates with tiled bed plate and lid and cottle walls. Unlocks tier 1 and most of the savings.
Sleeve height policy: drop-in lid with one tall sleeve per diameter, or standard heights plus spacer rings.
Worth doing either way: separate cores from plates, and design in jack screws and pry points.
